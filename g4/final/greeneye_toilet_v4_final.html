Glass Partition Benefits

Privacy: Frosted tempered glass (6mm) gives full visual privacy for the WC user while allowing light to pass through, keeping the small 9×6ft space feeling open.

Separation: Creates two distinct zones on the West wall — a private WC alcove (N half) and an entry corridor (S half) — without building a full brick wall that would eat into the 9ft width.

Ventilation preserved: Glass partition is floor-to-ceiling on the W wall but only extends ~3.5ft into the room (E-W direction). Air still flows freely across the top of the room for E→W cross-ventilation.

Light: Frosted glass diffuses light from the entry zone into the WC area and vice versa — no dark corners in a compact space.

Easy to clean: Glass is ideal for a wet toilet environment — no paint to peel, no plaster to absorb moisture.

Specifications — v4 Final Layout
WC on W wall · Glass partition · Urinal on S wall · All fixtures
WC — West Wall (North portion) Floor-mounted western commode with cistern against the West wall, user faces East (Vastu ✓). Occupies the North ~3ft of the West wall. Cistern gets cold water from main line via short drop along the NW corner. 4" soil pipe runs straight down the West wall to the drain exit — shortest possible soil pipe run. Health faucet + TP holder on the right side. Brands: Hindware, Parryware, Cera.
Glass Partition 6mm frosted tempered glass. Runs from the West wall inward ~3.5ft (E-W) at the 3ft mark (N-S) — exactly between the WC zone (North) and door zone (South). Floor-to-ceiling height. Creates a private WC alcove without eating into the 9ft room width. Allows light through. Easy to clean in wet environment. Fixed panel — no moving parts.
Door — West Wall (South portion) South 3ft of West wall. 0.5ft from S dividing wall, 2.5ft wide. Hinged on S jamb, swings inward toward East. Glass partition at the 3ft mark acts as the visual and spatial boundary between entry zone and WC zone. NW Vayu corner is inside the WC alcove — still free from door energy per Vastu.
Urinal — South Wall (center) Wall-hung ceramic urinal on solid S dividing wall, ~3.2ft from W end. User faces South. Immediately to the right upon entering — quick-access placement. 2" drain drops into S wall base. Concealed flush valve (plan 6"×6" recess during masonry). Brands: Hindware Flat Back, Parryware.
Handwash Counter — NE Corner 24"×18" granite/Kota counter against N wall (E portion). Table-top or under-mount basin. Single-lever mixer (hot + cold). Mirror on N wall. Full N-wall width now available since WC moved to W wall — counter can be generous in size.
Shower — SE Corner ~3ft × 2.4ft zone. 8" rain shower + handheld. Mixer with geyser hot + cold. Glass partition (W edge) separates wet/dry. Dedicated 3" floor drain. Anti-skid tiles. Floor slope 1:40.
Geyser — East Wall 15L wall-mounted at ~6ft height. Cold inlet from N wall main. Hot to handwash + shower. CPVC green hot piping. 2kW ISI. 15A socket + MCB. Brands: Bajaj, Racold, AO Smith.
Plumbing Advantage WC on the West wall means the 4" soil pipe runs straight down the wall and exits directly through the West wall — zero horizontal run. This is the most efficient soil pipe route possible. All other drains collect along the S wall base and converge at the W exit. The cold water main enters the same NW corner, keeping supply and drain on the same wall.
Ventilation Exhaust fan: W wall @7ft, above the WC zone (inside the glass partition alcove — exhausts directly from the most odour-prone area). Fresh air louvre: E wall @7ft. Cross-ventilation E→W pulls fresh air across the room and exhausts it right at the WC zone. Glass partition is floor-to-ceiling but only ~3.5ft long — air flows freely above and around it.
Layout Advantages Summary WC against W wall gives the shortest soil pipe run and keeps the center of the room completely open. Glass partition creates a private WC alcove without a masonry wall. Urinal on S wall gives immediate access from the door. Handwash in NE has the full N wall width. Shower in SE is isolated. Geyser on E wall feeds both hot-water fixtures. Every fixture is against a wall — maximum open floor area in the center.
